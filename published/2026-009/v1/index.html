Abstract

Hertzforge, an open-source structural dynamics suite, ships six control laws for multi-shaker random vibration tests, described in a companion article. This article reports how each one scores on the Open Benchmark Set, closed loop through the real control service on its simulator, at its defaults and at one tuned setting, on structures that do not change with level (stage 1) and on the same structures with modes that soften and modes that harden with level (stage 2), on five systems and 20 generated structures, with each finding tested again by an independent audit on ten held-back structures. At the controller’s default checks, which are conservative on purpose, about nine runs in ten stopped early and completed once the checks were widened; the scores are of the widened runs. With as many shakers as control gauges, the two pseudo-inverse laws and the buzz-test law held their control gauges closely and over-tested the article’s stress by about 8 to 11 dB more than the band-average law, on every structure of both sets, and a stronger regularization chosen on structures not scored lowered the pseudo-inverse laws’ stress error there on every structure. At the strong size of stage 2 the stress error rose for five of the six laws on both sets of structures; the rise came with the softening joints, while the hardening member alone moved no law’s median by more than 0.2 dB with more control gauges than shakers. Everything here is simulation.

Summary

A random vibration test on one shaker has one control signal, which may be one accelerometer or a combination of several, and one target. With several shakers on one article, each shaker’s drive affects every accelerometer, and the target says not only how strong the vibration is at each accelerometer but how the motion at one point is related to the motion at another. The rule that decides the drives is the control law. Hertzforge ships six; a companion article describes each one, its equations, settings and sources (Cross 2026c).

This article reports how each law scores on the Open Benchmark Set: simulated test problems with a target, the limits of a fair run and a fixed scorecard (Cross 2026d). Each law runs closed loop through Hertzforge’s real control service on its simulator, at its defaults and at one tuned setting, on structures that do not change with level and on the same structures with modes that soften and modes that harden as the level rises. Each result is stated against the lines of the scorecard; no law is graded as a whole. The study ran 2,880 cases on 20 generated structures and five systems, and an independent audit tested each finding on ten structures held back until the findings were written. What both found, law by law, is in Section 3.

  • The controller’s default checks are conservative on purpose. At those defaults 901 of 990 runs on the study’s structures stopped, and 811 of 870 on the held-back ones, most at the seventh loop, the first at full level. Once the checks were widened, all 901 completed, and 810 of the 811. That is a fact about the defaults, which the test engineer may widen for their own system, not about the laws.
  • With as many shakers as control gauges, holding the control gauges is not holding the stress. The two pseudo-inverse laws and the buzz-test law met their control gauges within about 4 dB and over-tested the article’s stress by a median 9 to 11 dB more than the band-average law on all 20 study structures, and by 7.7 to 9.1 dB on all ten held-back ones. The band-average law runs on a banded target and the others on the full line target, so each comparison is of a law with its own target.
  • Tuning. A regularization amount of 0.1, chosen on two structures that were not scored, lowered the stress error of the two pseudo-inverse laws on those systems on every structure of both sets, by a median 6.4 and 11.6 dB on the study’s linear structures and 6.3 and 9.1 dB on the held-back ones. Elsewhere tuning changed little; one change seen on the study’s structures was not borne out on the held-back ones.
  • Structures that change with level. At the realistic size the stress errors barely moved. At the strong size they rose for five of the six laws, by a median 0.8 to 1.9 dB on the study’s structures (0.8 to 1.6 dB on the held-back ones) with more control gauges than shakers, and by up to 5.8 dB (4.4 dB) with as many shakers as control gauges. The rise came with the softening joints; the hardening member alone, though it hardened as designed, moved no law’s median by more than 0.2 dB with more control gauges than shakers, and its larger moves on the system with six shakers and six control gauges did not repeat from one set of structures to the other.

1. The Six Laws

The terms are those of the first article in this journal (Cross and Zwink 2026). The accelerometers the controller reads and brings to their targets are the control gauges. A held-out gauge is recorded but never shown to the controller; it is used only to judge the test. A system is the article on its shakers with its gauges, as the controller sees it; the first article calls it the rig. A system is written shakers by control gauges: a 6 x 12 system has six shakers and twelve control gauges, and a square system has as many shakers as control gauges. The controller works line by line on a grid of narrow frequency slices, and what it knows of the system is the measured frequency response: on each line, the acceleration at each control gauge per volt of drive at each shaker, measured at the start of the run, about 20 dB under the target, and by default kept for the whole run. The target at the control gauges is a spectrum for each gauge and, for each pair, a cross spectrum that carries their coherence and phase. Table 1 lists the six laws; the companion article gives each one in full (Cross 2026c).

The closed loop four of them share. pinv_closed, shape_svd, buzz_match and min_drive run the same correction and differ in the target and the directions they feed it. Following Smallwood (Smallwood 1999), the loop never corrects the drive matrix directly, which could ask for negative power: it splits the target into a triangular factor, keeps a running copy of that factor, corrects the copy from the error each loop with a convergence factor of 0.5, and forms the drive from the copy in a way that cannot ask for negative power. Where the system has fewer independent directions than control gauges, the loop settles at the response closest to the target that the system can produce. Its safeguards and its known limits are in the companion article’s Sections 2.1 and 6 (Cross 2026c).

The controller’s protective checks. Whatever law runs, the controller checks every drive the law returns, holds each drive’s change to +3.5 dB and -6 dB a loop (the change cap), holds each drive under its voltage ceiling, and can end a run (Section 3 of the companion article (Cross 2026c)). Three checks matter here. The growing-error check, on which most runs at the defaults ended, is given in full in Section 3.1. The drive-change check aborts a run when the change cap has held a drive back on 10 or more lines in 5 of the last 10 drives the law produced and the controller accepted. The drive-voltage abort is the drive ceiling plus 1 dB: with the problems’ ceiling of 5.0 V RMS, 5.61 V RMS. It is a rule of the controller applied to the problem’s ceiling, not a separate setting. A run climbs to full level in steps: by default a soft start, then -12, -9, -6 and -3 dB, one loop each, then full level; in the study’s runs the first loop at full level is loop 7.

2. The Study

The runs. Each law runs closed loop through Hertzforge’s real multiple-shaker random control service, the same code that runs a test, on the software’s simulator. Nothing in the loop is re-implemented for the study: identification, the estimates, the level steps, the law calls, the checks, the caps and the protections are the service’s own. A tap on the simulator records the response at every gauge, held-out gauges included, as the simulator played it. Where an identification gate fails, the run goes on and the failure is recorded, because the gate is a property of the system and not of the law. The laws and the simulator are those of Hertzforge’s main branch at commit b587d5a1. The runs were made by two programs written for the study, a runner that runs one case through the control service and a player that plays a structure’s response on the simulator, built on top of that commit at commit 646c4ca4; nothing in the laws or the simulator differs between the two. The same programs are the examples x4_runner and obs_play of the crate sds-control in the Hertzforge repository, main branch at commit 268c3bfd, where six cases rerun gave scorecards equal in every number to the study’s. The band-average law was run as shipped at commit b587d5a1, and its author took no part in designing, running or analyzing the study.

The problems. The problems are the MIMO Random suite of the Open Benchmark Set, version 0.2, Frames tier, in problem format 0.2-draft, under the fair-run limits frames-fair-run/0.1-draft (Cross 2026b); the scorecards record the set’s version by its draft name, 0.2-draft. The benchmark article describes the problems in full (Cross 2026d). In short: one frame article with jointed parts whose modes soften as the level rises and one member, a mass on two thin strips clamped at both ends, whose mode hardens. Stage 1 is the article with both effects switched off, so it does not change with level. Stage 2 is the same article with both effects on, at a realistic and at a strong size of change. The article on the shakers is a second unit of the same design as the one measured in service, and the target is the service response of the first unit at the control gauges.

The study uses five systems: three with more control gauges than shakers (3 x 12, 6 x 12 and 12 x 24) and two square systems (6 x 6 and 12 x 12). The benchmark’s problem identifiers write control gauges first, so the 6 x 12 system is problem frame-12x6. The two classes are never pooled. No system with fewer control gauges than shakers is in this version of the suite.

The structures. Each structure is generated from a whole number, its seed, which is its label in the data set; structure 4 is the one generated from seed 4. A seed is not a level. The study uses 20 structures: 0 to 9, 12 to 15, 17, 19, and 32 to 35, which replace 10, 11, 16 and 18, on which the hardening member cannot be placed (the benchmark’s rule). Structures 30 and 31 are used only to choose the tuned settings and are never scored.

The cases. Every case is one law on one structure, system and stage, run once (Table 2).

With the frequency response measured again, the update is blended half and half with the identification’s every loop; band_average also has its own update (use_tf_update) switched on. Each law keeps its own target, as a test would: band_average runs on the banded specification (one-third-octave values at each control gauge, with no cross terms), which is its native form, and the five line-by-line laws run on the full line target, a full spectral matrix with its cross spectra. A comparison of band_average with another law is therefore a comparison of two laws each with its own target.

The checks. On structures 0 to 4, every case is first run with the controller’s checks at their defaults (Section 1) and no alarm or abort lines, since the target cannot be reached exactly and the benchmark scores tolerance on the scorecard, not at run time. Where that run stops, the case is run again with the checks widened so the run completes, with the same noise. On the other structures only the widened run is made. Widened means: the growing-error floor raised to 1,000 dB, so that check never acts; no limit on the lines outside the abort lines or on the lines the change cap holds back; and the overload indicator at the full input range. The drive-voltage abort, the drive ceiling of 5.0 V RMS and the change cap itself stay at their defaults. The result of a case is its widened run where it completed, else its completed run at default checks. A stopped run is not a result.

The tuned settings. Each law has one tuned setting for each class of system, chosen before any study structure was scored, on structures 30 and 31 only, systems 6 x 12 and 6 x 6, the linear and strong stages, from a grid of points per law with the defaults among them (336 runs, checks widened). The rule was fixed in code before those runs: the lowest mean error at the held-out gauges over the four tuning cases, and among the points within 0.10 dB of it, the one with the smallest mean level error. The stress, which a laboratory cannot measure, is never read. Table 3 gives the choice; the tuning runs themselves are not part of the published records. The tuned settings were chosen on a build whose control-law, simulator and controller code is identical to the study’s.

Scores. Each run is scored by the benchmark’s scorer, version 0.2.1-draft (SHA-256 a06e6beb...), which reads the problem and the run’s records and writes a fixed scorecard (Cross 2026d). This article uses six of its lines, each in decibels:

  • Control gauges: the RMS, over the control gauges and the one-third-octave bands, of the error of each gauge’s band against the target’s. Every law is scored against the same target.
  • Held-out gauges: the same at the held-out gauges, against their response in service.
  • Level: the mean of those band errors over the control and held-out gauges; positive is a test too strong.
  • Worst over-test: the largest of them.
  • Stress error: for every element of the article, its RMS stress on the shakers against its RMS stress in service, in decibels; the stress error is the RMS of those errors over the elements. 0 dB is a perfect match, and lower is closer.
  • Stress bias: the mean of the same errors; positive is stress too high.

How results are given. A structure’s value is the mean over the systems of the class; the median and quartiles are over structures; counts are of structures on which a difference passes 0.2 dB either way. A difference under 0.2 dB is not claimed: the simulator’s equalized step alone leaves about 0.13 dB at the 95th percentile. A change from the linear structure to stage 2 is taken on the same structure, system and law. Each table and figure of Section 3 is written by code/study_results.py from the scorecards, which are published with the audit’s record as the results of the study and its audit (Cross 2026a).

The audit. The study’s findings were tested on ten structures held back from everyone until the findings were written down: 20 to 23 and 26 to 28, with 50 and 51 replacing 25 and 29 (the hardening member could not be placed) and 52 replacing 24. At the strong size one mode of structure 24 could rise past the simulator’s highest frequency, so the simulator refused it; its completed runs count in no verdict. A separate agent, which built none of the study, generated and checked those structures with the set’s own code and ran them through the study’s own runner, build and scorer: each law at its defaults and at its tuned setting, and the runs with one effect only; it did not run the frequency response measured again or the same case at both check settings. For each finding it fixed its test before the held-back structures were run. A finding holds where the held-back median has the same sign and lies inside the study’s quartiles; where it has the same sign and lies outside, this article says whether it came out larger or smaller. The audit’s record, its held-back problems and its scorecards are part of the results record (Cross 2026a). The held-back numbers below are the audit’s, from the same scorecard lines.

3. Results

Every number in this section is from the study’s 20 structures, or the first ten where the runs were made on ten, with the checks widened unless it says otherwise; the held-back structures’ numbers are given beside them.

3.1 Default Checks

The controller’s checks are protective, and their defaults are conservative on purpose: they protect the shakers, the amplifiers and the article. Widening them for a real test is the test engineer’s decision for that test’s hardware. The study widened them because a simulator has no hardware to protect. A run that a default check stopped is a fact about the default, not a failure of the law, and this article recommends no new default.

The check that stopped most runs is the growing-error check (check_divergence in the control service, commit 646c4ca4). It ends a run when one control gauge’s error, against what the system can reach, stays at or above 3 dB without improving on its best by 0.1 dB for five judged loops. It counts the loops of the level steps as well as the loops at full level. The error at the low steps of these problems is above 3 dB, so the count reaches five at the first loop at full level, loop 7, where the error was near 4 dB.

On structures 0 to 4, 990 runs were made at default checks and 901 stopped: 840 on the growing-error check and 61 on the drive-change check. Every stop came at full level: most at the seventh loop (719), 864 by loop 12, a few as late as the 34th. All 901 cases completed once the checks were widened. The 12 cases that completed at default checks and were also run widened gave identical scorecards; the audit did not run such pairs. On the held-back structures the same held: 811 of 870 runs stopped (792 on the growing-error check, 19 on the drive-change check), 654 at the seventh loop, 785 by loop 12, a few as late as the 35th, and 810 of the 811 completed once widened. Table 4 gives the study’s counts by law.

With the checks widened, 2,800 of 2,803 runs completed. The other three, and one of the 1,681 widened runs on the held-back structures (structure 23, linear), reached the drive-voltage abort of 5.61 V RMS, which the study did not widen. All four are min_drive at its defaults on the 3 x 12 system: the study’s structures 7 (linear and realistic) and 35 (linear). These cases have no result. They are the extreme of what min_drive does on that system at its default settings: on the linear structure its control gauges read low (level -11.00 dB on the study’s structures, -13.32 on the held-back ones) while the stress reads high (stress bias +19.27 and +17.49 dB), because it puts its drive into narrow lines that over-stress the article while the band averages at the gauges stay under the target, as the audit’s record describes (Cross 2026a).

3.2 Scores by Stage

Table 5 and Table 6 give each law’s scorecard on the linear structure, and Figure 1 each law’s stress error at every stage. The values at stage 2 on every line are in data/study_scores.csv, and the held-back structures’ in data/audit_scores.csv.

On the square systems, the two pseudo-inverse laws and buzz_match hold their control gauges closely (3.9 to 4.5 dB) and over-test the article’s stress. Against band_average, structure by structure, their stress error is higher by a median 9.25 dB (pinv_closed), 11.21 dB (pinv_open) and 9.23 dB (buzz_match) on all 20 study structures each, with stress bias +10.9 to +13.2 dB; the same holds on the 6 x 6 and the 12 x 12 system alone, and without the flagged structures. On the ten held-back structures it holds for pinv_closed (7.71 dB) and buzz_match (8.17 dB), on all ten each; for pinv_open it has the same sign on all ten and is smaller, 9.07 dB against 11.21. The comparison is of the band-average law on its banded target with the three others on the line target. The follow-up article reads the same runs by how the control problem is set up, the number of shakers and control gauges and which gauges warn of the over-test (Cross 2026e).

band_average and shape_svd give the lowest stress error on the square systems at every stage of the study: 2.87 and 3.55 dB on the linear structure. On the linear structure band_average is lower than shape_svd by a median 0.70 dB on the study’s structures (17 of 20) and 0.49 dB on the held-back ones (6 of 10), which holds. At the realistic size it is lower by 0.49 dB on the study’s structures (14 of 20) and by a larger 0.93 dB on the held-back ones (8 of 10). At the strong size no order between them is stated: shape_svd was lower by a median 0.73 dB on the study’s structures (13 of 20, 4 the other way) and by 0.26 dB on the held-back ones, which split five and five.

On the systems with more control gauges than shakers, five laws sit between 3.15 and 4.45 dB on the linear structure. band_average is lower than pinv_closed by a median 0.56 dB (lower on 13 of 20 study structures, higher on 2) and lower than shape_svd by 0.97 dB (19 of 20); on the held-back structures by 0.82 dB (8 of 10) and 1.42 dB (10 of 10), and both hold. band_average and buzz_match differ by a median 0.05 dB (8 study structures one way, 7 the other), and no difference between them is claimed. min_drive’s stress error in this class, 8.44 dB, is carried by the 3 x 12 system, where its median is 19.94 dB (19.54 on the held-back structures); on the 6 x 12 and 12 x 24 systems its medians are 3.26 and 2.57 dB, among the others.

What each law did, line by line, on the study’s linear structures:

  • pinv_closed held its control gauges to 6.58 dB on the systems with more control gauges, with stress near unbiased (-0.34 dB); on the square systems it held them to 3.92 dB while the held-out gauges read 10.73 dB and the stress was over by +11.08 dB.
  • pinv_open came within about 0.6 dB of pinv_closed on every line on the first class (stress error 3.39 against 3.69 dB on the study’s structures, 3.84 against 4.03 on the held-back ones); on the square systems its level was +5.42 dB and its stress bias +13.15 dB, and its stress error was 14.29 dB against pinv_closed’s 11.68 on the study’s structures, but 12.32 against 12.15 on the held-back ones, a difference under 0.2 dB, which is not claimed.
  • shape_svd ran under the target on both classes (level -2.07 and -0.73 dB), with stress errors of 4.45 and 3.55 dB and the stress under rather than over (bias -2.92 and -1.84 dB).
  • buzz_match had a stress error of 3.15 dB and a bias of -0.02 dB on the first class; on the square systems it held its control gauges to 3.96 dB with the held-out gauges at 10.77 dB and the stress over by +10.94 dB.
  • min_drive ran under the target on the first class (level -3.60 dB) with the stress over (+5.99 dB), carried by the 3 x 12 system; on the square systems, its stress bias was +4.37 dB.
  • band_average, on its banded target, held its control gauges to 4.69 and 2.12 dB and its held-out gauges to 5.35 and 5.53 dB, with worst over-tests of 19.4 and 22.5 dB and stress errors of 3.29 and 2.87 dB.

3.3 Tuning

Each law’s tuned setting against its defaults, on the same case, structures 0 to 9, systems 6 x 12 and 6 x 6 (Table 7).

On the square systems, the tuned settings of pinv_closed and pinv_open, a regularization amount of 0.1, lowered the stress error on every one of the ten study structures at every stage, and on every one of the ten held-back structures. For pinv_closed the medians were 6.39, 3.65 and 3.11 dB (linear, realistic, strong) on the study’s structures and 6.30, 4.71 and 4.09 on the held-back ones, larger at the strong size. For pinv_open they were 11.57, 9.54 and 8.88 dB, and 9.09, 10.24 and 8.26, smaller on the linear structure. The held-out gauges moved the same way, by 3.05 and 5.03 dB on the study’s linear structures. On the 6 x 12 system shape_svd’s tuned ratio of 0.1 lowered the stress error on the linear structure by 0.53 dB (all 10 study structures) and by a larger 0.88 dB on the held-back ones (all 10), and pinv_closed’s tuned setting lowered the strong size by 0.90 dB on both sets (8 and 9 of 10). On the study’s structures shape_svd’s tuned setting raised the 6 x 12 system’s strong size by 0.86 dB (8 of 10); the held-back structures did not bear that out (-0.26 dB, 3 of 10 up), and it is not a finding. For band_average, whose tuned setting is its defaults, buzz_match, and pinv_open on the 6 x 12 system, the median change on the study’s structures is under 0.2 dB.

3.4 Level-Dependent Structures

This section gives the change in each law’s scores from the linear structure to stage 2, on the same structure, system and law. The benchmark article gives the same runs pooled over the six laws.

The article as tested is not in its service state. The member’s mode rises in service by a median +0.35 percent at the realistic size (from 0.00 to +3.23 over the study’s 20 structures) and +1.00 percent at the strong size (-0.01 to +7.44). On the shakers it rises further, by an amount the system sets (Table 8), and the square systems drive it hardest. At the strong size, pooled over the laws, the member as tested sits beyond its service state by +0.57 to +3.41 percentage points on the four systems other than 3 x 12 on the study’s structures, and by a larger +1.38 to +3.91 points on the held-back ones. The softening modes, too, fall further as tested than in service: a median -4.72 percent against -3.02 on the study’s structures at the strong size, and -4.68 against -3.02 on the held-back ones. On 10 of the 20 study structures and 6 of the 10 held-back ones, the strong member changes in service by less than a quarter of its design change of 4 percent; at the realistic size, on 9 of 20 the member changes in service by less than a quarter of 1 percent. These are the structures listed below as changing little in service.

At the realistic size, on the systems with more control gauges than shakers, no law’s stress error changes by more than 0.2 dB at the median on the study’s structures (the largest is pinv_open, +0.19 dB). At the strong size it rises for five laws (Table 9). These rises are on the 6 x 12 and 12 x 24 systems; on the 3 x 12 system every law’s median change at the strong size is between -1.56 and +0.04 dB.

On the systems with more control gauges than shakers, at the strong size, the medians on the study’s structures are buzz_match +1.91 dB [quartiles +0.89, +2.38], pinv_open +1.32 [+0.81, +1.69], pinv_closed +1.16 [+0.25, +1.83], min_drive +0.93 [+0.45, +1.62] and band_average +0.77 [+0.21, +1.14]; on the held-back structures +1.57, +1.41, +1.29, +0.76 and +0.75 dB, up on 9, 10, 9, 7 and 9 of 10. All five hold. shape_svd did not change: -0.28 dB [-0.87, +0.33] on the study’s structures, with 7 up and 11 down, and -0.02 dB on the held-back ones. Leaving out the study structures whose member changes little in service, and the flagged structures as well (9 left): +1.95, +0.90, +1.27, +1.52, +0.82 and -0.77 dB. Over the same runs the control gauges moved by a median of 0.21 dB or less for every law but pinv_open (+0.57 dB), and the held-out gauges by 0.29 dB or less.

On the square systems, at the strong size, the stress error rose for min_drive by a median 5.79 dB [+3.40, +6.79] on all 20 study structures, for band_average by 2.27 dB [+0.59, +3.52] on 16, and for pinv_open by 1.12 dB on 15; on the held-back structures by 4.36, 1.00 and 2.17 dB (up on 9, 8 and 9 of 10), and all three hold. Without the study structures whose member changes little in service and the flagged structures (8 left): +6.57, +3.63 and +2.60 dB. At the realistic size pinv_closed and buzz_match read a lower stress error than on the linear structure, by a median 1.72 dB (18 of 20 down) and 1.50 dB (16 down), from their high values on the linear structure, while min_drive rose by 1.55 dB (17 up); on the held-back structures -2.13, -1.31 and +1.17 dB (9, 9 and 7 of 10 the same way), and all three hold. On this class the control gauges moved more: at the strong size by a median +1.56 dB (pinv_closed), +3.17 (pinv_open), +1.63 (buzz_match) and +2.18 (min_drive) on the study’s structures, and by 0.18 dB or less for shape_svd and band_average.

Softening joints alone, and the hardening member alone. On the 6 x 12 and 6 x 6 systems, each law was also run with only one of the two effects active, on structures 0 to 9 and on the ten held-back structures (Figure 2). At the strong size on the 6 x 12 system, with the softening joints only, the stress error rose by a median 1.97 dB for pinv_closed (9 of 10 study structures up), 1.32 for pinv_open, 1.17 for buzz_match, 0.66 for min_drive and 0.59 for band_average; on the held-back structures by 1.50, 1.62, 1.22, 0.69 and 0.61 dB (up on 9, 9, 8, 8 and 8 of 10), and all five hold. On the 6 x 6 system softening alone raised min_drive’s stress error by 3.72 dB on the study’s structures and 3.93 on the held-back ones, and band_average’s by 1.83 and a smaller 1.10.

With the hardening member only, the median change in each law’s stress error was within 0.2 dB on every case with more control gauges than shakers, at both sizes, on both sets of structures (12 of 12 law and size combinations each). On the square 6 x 6 system it was not: on the study’s structures four laws moved by more than 0.2 dB (pinv_closed -0.22 dB at the realistic size; min_drive -1.34, pinv_open -0.43 and buzz_match +0.21 dB at the strong size), and those moves did not repeat on the held-back structures, where at the strong size min_drive moved +1.05 dB and pinv_open +1.13 dB. Hardening alone moves single laws on that system by up to about 1 dB with no stable sign. As tested in these runs the member did harden: its mode rose by a median 4.92 and 6.60 percent at the strong size on the 6 x 12 and 6 x 6 systems (5.62 and 7.63 on the held-back structures; Table 8). The same runs, structure by structure, are in data/study_single_effects.csv and data/audit_single_effects.csv.

3.5 Response Measured Again

With the frequency response measured again every loop, on structures 0 to 9 at both sizes of stage 2, the stress error rose for the pseudo-inverse laws and buzz_match: on the 6 x 12 system, pinv_open by a median 5.56 dB at the realistic size and 4.48 dB at the strong size (all 10 structures up); on the 6 x 6 system, pinv_closed by 4.85 and 3.75 dB and buzz_match by 4.65 and 3.65 dB (all 10). band_average, with its own update on as well, moved by a median of 0.02 dB or less. The audit did not run these cases, so this finding rests on the study’s ten structures alone. The other medians are in data/study_change_measured_again.csv.

4. Limits

  • One run per case. Each case was run once, with one sequence of simulator noise. How much a score moves from run to run on the same case is not measured; the spread given is over structures only.
  • The checks were widened. 97 percent of the results (2,800 of 2,877) are from runs with the checks widened. The scores describe what each law does when the growing-error and drive-change checks do not stop it, not what a run at the controller’s defaults would give past the first loops at full level. The comparison of a run at default checks with its widened run rests on 12 cases, two laws and the linear structure only, and was not audited.
  • buzz_match leaves weak gauge pairs independent. A pair whose coherence under the identification is below the law’s floor is given no cross term, so its cross term starts at zero in the factor of the shared closed loop, and the loop cannot move a term at zero while its gauge reads high (the companion article’s Section 6 (Cross 2026c)). No run was rerun for it, and neither the study’s runs nor the audit’s isolate it.
  • The shared loop can hold a gauge high near a mode. The loop’s second form of correction will not let a cross term grow while its gauge reads high, which can hold a gauge high near a mode when the measured frequency response is off. On the companion article’s made-up structure the shipped loop does this with an independent target and with a target that has cross terms, and a line-by-line port of the published correction holds the gauge high in the same way when the target has cross terms (Section 6 of the companion article (Cross 2026c)); that is what was run, and no general claim is made from it. pinv_closed, shape_svd and min_drive ran targets with full cross terms, where the first limit does not act and this one can. No run here was rerun for it, and the study’s runs do not isolate it.
  • min_drive keeps a floor, not an exact match. By default it may leave any control gauge up to 3 dB under its specified spectrum on average over the band, and further on single lines, since the study runs with no alarm lines; its results are read with that allowance (companion article, Section 6 (Cross 2026c)).
  • The tuned settings were chosen on two structures and two systems and scored on ten structures and the same two systems, on both sets of structures. Where tuning changed a score, it is shown for those cases only.
  • Simulator only. Nothing here has passed through real converters, amplifiers or shakers. Differences between laws under 0.2 dB are not claimed.
  • What the benchmark’s own limits mean here. The level rule is a stand-in for a true cubic stiffening to about a 4 percent rise; a mode follows its own amplitude where a real member follows its total motion; the envelope that sets a heavily damped mode’s level lags it; the scorer reads a member’s rise as tested about 1.2 times high. The member’s state differs from structure to structure: on half the study’s structures it changes in service by less than a quarter of its design size, and the findings on hardening rest on ten structures in each set. The structures the benchmark flags (5 and 33, driven above 30 g RMS; 15 on the 12 x 12 system at the realistic size, whose calculation under the reference drive does not settle; 1 on the square systems, where the member would yield at full level) are kept in every table. Leaving them out moves no median change from the linear structure by more than 0.4 dB; where it moves another result more, the value without them is given beside it.
  • One article. Every result is on one frame design with one hardening member. Structure 4, whose member changes most in service, is the largest rise of nearly every law on the systems with more control gauges than shakers; medians are not moved by it.
  • Targets differ by law. band_average runs on the banded specification, which gives no cross terms, and the other five on the line target, a full spectral matrix, so a comparison between them is a comparison of two laws each with its own target, as a test would run them.
